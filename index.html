What the prompt looks like

Sample prompt

You should spend about 20 minutes on this task. suggested time

You have received an email from your English-speaking friend, Anna, who is planning to visit your city next month. Read part of her email: situation → who is the reader?

“I'm so excited about my trip! Could you help me with a few things? What's the weather like in your city in November? Which places do you think I should visit? And where would be a good, not too expensive place to stay?”

Write an email of reply to Anna, and: 3 required points

  • tell her about the weather in November,
  • suggest two places she should visit, and
  • recommend a place to stay.

Write at least 120 words. Do NOT include your name or any addresses. ≥120 words · no name

Reading the prompt
  • The reader sets the register: a friend → informal; a teacher, manager, company, stranger → formal.
  • 3 bullet points = 3 body paragraphs. The prompt may be a formal "feedback" type (what you found most useful · one thing to improve · would you attend again) – it's still 3 points.
  • No name, no address: the sign-off is just Best wishes, / Yours sincerely,.

Splitting the 20 minutes

2′
  • Read the prompt, underline the 3 points
  • Decide the register
  • Jot down one detail per point
15′
  • Write using the 5-part frame
  • Each point: answer + detail + reason
3′
  • Greeting – sign-off match the register
  • Tense, s/es
  • Word count (~8–9 words/line handwritten)

Formal or informal?

InformalFormal
Writing toFriends, family, a pen pal (Anna, Ben, Jenny)A manager, a company, a school, a stranger, an organiser (Mr Lewis, Ms Wilson, Sir/Madam)
GreetingDear Anna, / Hi Ben,Dear Mr Lewis, / Dear Ms Wilson, (name known) · Dear Sir or Madam, (name unknown)
Sign-offBest wishes, / All the best, / Love, / Take care,Yours sincerely, (name known) · Yours faithfully, (Sir or Madam) · Kind regards,
LanguageContractions (I'm, can't), short sentences, feelings, phrasal verbs, exclamation marksNo contractions, full sentences, indirect politeness (Could you… / I would be grateful if…), no slang
Reason for writingThanks for your email. It was great to hear from you.Thank you for your email. I am writing to give you my feedback on…
Semi-formal

A former teacher, a host, a doctor, an older acquaintance → semi-formal: Dear Mrs Green, … Best regards,; polite, can be friendly, few contractions. When unsure: choose formal – it's the safer option.

The 5-part frame

1 · Greeting1 line
Dear Anna, · Dear Mr Lewis, · Dear Sir or Madam,
2 · Opening1–2 sentences
Thanks / reason for writing: Thanks for your email. It was great to hear that… · Thank you for your email. I am writing to…
3 · Body3 paragraphs × 2–3 sentences
Point 1 First of all, … → answer + detail + reason
Point 2 As for …, / Regarding …, / Secondly, …
Point 3 Finally, … (don't skip point 3 just because time is short!)
4 · Closing1–2 sentences
Let me know if you need anything else. I can't wait to see you! · I look forward to hearing from you.
5 · Sign-off1 line
Best wishes, · Love, · Yours sincerely, · Yours faithfully, — no name

All together ≈ 11–13 sentences ≈ 130–150 words.

Common mistakes

MistakeHow to fix it
  • Formal letter opens with "Hi"
  • Informal one opens with "Dear Sir or Madam"
Set the register in the first line and keep it to the sign-off:
  • Dear Mr Lewis → Yours sincerely
  • Hi Ben → Best wishes
Missing the sign-off line, or including your real name/addressThe prompt forbids names. Close with exactly one line:
  • Best wishes,
  • Yours sincerely,
Missing 1 of the 3 points (usually point 3, from running out of time)
  • One short paragraph per point
  • Cover all 3 points first, add detail afterwards
Copying the prompt's own wordingParaphrase it:
  • What's the weather like? → The weather in November is usually cool and dry.
A one-line answer: "Yes, you should visit the museum."Answer + 1 detail + 1 reason:
  • …the Central Museum, which shows the history of the region – it's free on Sundays.
One solid block of text, no paragraph breaks
  • New line after the greeting
  • One paragraph per point
  • The closing line and sign-off on their own lines
Too long (>180 words) → eats into Task 2 time
  • 120–150 words is enough for full marks
  • Stop once you've covered the 3 points + a closing line
Wrong tense: I will visit you last week
  • Something that already happened → past tense
  • A plan → will / be going to
  • Habits and facts → present simple
